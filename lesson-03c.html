Lesson 03 – Animating in Harmony

schedule Duration: 4 hours movie 24 FPS · Animated on ones/twos/threes

info About this Lesson

Time to jump right into animating in Toon Boom Harmony. In this lesson you will learn how to start, create and export an animated project in Harmony. Whether this is your first time opening up Toon boom Harmony or you are a seasoned Pro, we will show you a quick warm-up exercise to get you animating in Harmony right away. This animation will be completed at 24 Frames Per Second and will be animated on ones. We will be making an animated scene where two dots are chasing or playing with each other as they cross the camera in Harmony.

menu_book Topics

  • arrow_forwardAnimating on Ones: An animation technique of drawing on every single frame of an animated sequence. Animating on the ones creates a smooth animated sequence, but is extremely time consuming.
  • arrow_forwardAnimating on Twos: An animation technique of drawing on every second frame of an animated sequence. The following empty frame is filled with the previous drawing frame. Most classical animations and animated television shows animate on the twos. Animating on the twos provides a fluid animation and is less time consuming than animating on Ones.
  • arrow_forwardAnimating on Threes: An animation technique of drawing on every third frame in an animated sequence. Most Anime uses this technique, which makes it less time consuming to animate complex characters on a tighter budget.
  • arrow_forwardExporting: Compiles an animated project into a playable video format which universal digital players can play. Harmony can export animated scenes into video files, GIFs, images and more.
  • arrow_forwardFrames Per Second (FPS): An animated sequence must have at least 2 frames, but to create an optical illusion it is recommended that 24 frames are used to create one second of animation. The default frame rate in Harmony is 24 Frames Per Second.
  • arrow_forwardFrame Rate: When you create an animation, the images you animate must move fluidly to produce the correct effect. If they don't, you will have a jumpy animation, which won't look good. To get a fluid movement between the drawings, you need several images per second. You need a minimum of 12 images per second, any slower than that and the animation becomes jumpy. An image can also be called a frame; therefore the frame rate is the number of frames you play per second in your animation.
  • arrow_forwardFrame Size: The size of your project is the width and height of your camera frame in pixels. A pixel is the smallest element of an image displayed on a monitor or TV screen. Pixels are composed of three colours (RGB—Red, Green, Blue).
  • arrow_forwardStraight Ahead Animation: One of the Principles of Animation. It is completed by creating drawings on numerous frames in sequence along a timeline to create an optical illusion.

layers Harmony Topics

  • Xstage file: Native computer extension for Toon Boom Harmony.
  • User Interface: Knowing how to manage the Toon Boom Harmony Advanced interface helps you to work efficiently and organize your workspace conveniently. There are a series of views and toolbars you can use as you perform different operations. Usually, each person has a specific way of working in the interface and prefer particular views and toolbars. In this chapter, you will learn about the main elements of the interface and how to manage them.
  • Colour View: The Colour view is where you create colours and palettes and import existing palettes into your project. The Colour view is also necessary for drawing, painting and creating colour styling.
  • Timeline View: To set your animation timing, you will mostly work with the Timeline and Xsheet views. It's extremely useful to become familiar with the Timeline view, how it works, and its interface. The Timeline view is the main view used when adjusting the timing of drawings, adding keyframes and ordering layers. It displays layers, effects, sounds, keyframe values, scene length, layer names, drawings, keyframes, timing, and frames.
  • Tools Toolbar: The Tools toolbar contains the main tools you need to work in Harmony. In the default workspace, this toolbar is located on the left edge of the application window.
  • Camera View: The Camera view is the centre of operations in Harmony. In this view, you can draw, paint, animate, set up your scene, manipulate objects, open symbols and preview your animation. The Camera view also has a top and bottom toolbar that you can use to navigate in the view, change the display mode or go up your symbol hierarchy.
  • Tool Properties: The Tool Properties view contains the most common options and operations related to the currently selected tool. When you select a tool from the Tools toolbar, the Tool Properties view updates.
  • Onion Skin: Displays the previous and next frames of an animation. Useful when animating cut-out and paperless characters.
  • Exporting: Harmony supports several formats for all your rendering needs.

draw Task

Hands-On Activity – Fun with Dots

  • Begin by watching and taking notes on Your First Animation video (above) and the topics presented in this lesson. Continue to take notes in your sketchbook/notebook.
  • Download and review the Shortcuts PDF in the Lesson Files.
  • Animate and complete your first animation. Save and export your Harmony file as YourName_FunWithDots in your Documents folder. Submit your animated video using the Submit File section at the bottom of this page.

Step 1

  • Launch Toon Boom Harmony.
  • In the Welcome Window, create a new scene called: Dots
  • Browse and set the location to your Documents Folder.
  • Keep the Camera Size at the default HDTV_1080p24 1080p (1920 × 1080 pixels and the frame rate is 24 frames per second).
  • Click Create Scene.
  • Select the Brush Tool brush.
  • Hint: Quick overview of the Harmony UI: Tools (left), Camera View (center) next to Drawing View, Timeline (bottom), Colour view (right of Camera View), Tool Properties (above Colour view).

Step 2

  • Go to the Timeline and rename the existing Layer to “Dot 1”.
  • Ensure the Brush Tool brush is selected. Choose black in the Colour view and place the cursor in the Camera view.
  • Hold down the O key to adjust brush size by dragging in the Camera view.
  • Draw a dot along the left side of the camera box. A small rectangle appears next to the Dot 1 layer indicating artwork on the first frame.
  • Use the , (comma) and . (period) keys to toggle between previous and next frames.
  • Enable Onion Skin layers to see previous frame positions in red.
  • Animate the dot across the Camera View using a dynamic path over 30 frames.
  • Vary spacing and size between frames. Try elongating some dots for a sense of movement.
  • At frame 30, click stop in the Timeline Panel to end the scene. Scrub the playhead to review.
  • Adjust positioning and size of the dots on any frames as needed.
  • Enable loop on the Timeline Panel and click play_arrow to watch the animation.

Step 3

  • Save your work (File → Save or CMD/CTRL + S).
  • Add a new Drawing Layer named “Dot 2”.
  • In the Colour view, pick a new colour for your Brush tool (avoid red/green which are used by Onion Skin).
  • Animate Dot 2 to chase/play with Dot 1 across the Camera View.
  • Review by scrubbing the Playhead or pressing play_arrow in the Timeline Panel.
  • Double the duration to 60 frames and apply animating on the twos for added fluidity.
  • In the Timeline, Shift-select frame 1 on “Dot 1” and frame 30 on “Dot 2” to highlight all frames.
  • Right-click frame 30 → Exposure → Set Exposure to 2 (each drawing extends to two frames).
  • Select frame 60 and click stop in the Timeline Panel. Play your animation.
  • Hint: Harmony exports with a transparent background (often black in players). Add a Colour Card for a background colour as needed.

Step 4

  • In the Timeline, add a Colour Card via the add icon, then choose a colour via the mixer.
  • Review your animated scene.
  • Export: File → Export → Animated GIF. Choose your Documents folder as the destination.
  • Test the exported animation in a browser.
  • Submit your work.

Congrats, you animated your first scene in this course. Don’t forget to submit your exported animation.
