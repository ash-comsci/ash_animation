Section Title 1

Key Topics
  • animation Animating on Ones

    An animation technique of drawing on every single frame of an animated sequence. Animating on the ones creates a smooth animated sequence, but is extremely time consuming.

  • animation Animating on Twos

    An animation technique of drawing on every second frame of an animated sequence. The following empty frame is filled with the previous drawing frame. Most classical animations and animated television shows animate on the twos. Animating on the twos provides a fluid animation and is less time consuming than animating on Ones.

  • animation Animating on Threes

    An animation technique of drawing on every third frame in an animated sequence. Most Anime uses this technique, which makes it less time consuming to animate complex characters on a tighter budget.

  • movie Exporting

    Compiles an animated project into a playable video format which universal digital players can play. Harmony can export animated scenes into video files, GIFs, images and more.

  • speed Frames Per Second (FPS)

    An animated sequence must have at least 2 frames, but to create an optical illusion it is recommended that 24 frames are used to create one second of animation. The default frame rate in Harmony is 24 Frames Per Second.

  • settings Frame Rate

    When you create an animation, the images you animate must move fluidly to produce the correct effect. If they don't, you will have a jumpy animation, which won't look good. To get a fluid movement between the drawings, you need several images per second. You need a minimum of 12 images per second, any slower than that and the animation becomes jumpy. An image can also be called a frame; therefore the frame rate is the number of frames you play per second in your animation.

  • aspect_ratio Frame Size

    The size of your project is the width and height of your camera frame in pixels. You may not be familiar with the term 'pixel', but you have probably seen it when you magnify a digital photograph. A pixel is the smallest element of an image displayed on a monitor or TV screen. Pixel, which is short for picture element, is a single point in a graphic image. It is a small sample of an image, a dot, a square or a very small section made out of smooth filtering. If you zoom in close enough on a digital image, you will see the pixels; they look like small squares of different colours and intensity. Pixels are composed of three colours (RGB—Red, Green, Blue).

  • trending_up Straight Ahead Animation

    This is one of the Principles of Animation. It is completed by creating drawings on numerous frames in sequence along a timeline to create an optical illusion.
