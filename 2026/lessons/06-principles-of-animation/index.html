The Principles of Animation

Work through the lesson notes, demonstrations, tasks and activities below. Use the navigation above to move through the course.

Main lesson video

About this Lesson:

The 12 Principles of Animation are guidelines that were developed from the 1930s and eventually introduced in the book The Illusion of Life by Disney animators Ollie Johnston and Frank Thomas in 1981. They have been developed over time by multiple masters of animation. These guidelines help animators create believable and solid performances in their work, and can be applied to multiple forms of animation ranging from 2D and 3D to stop motion. It is important that any animator, whether amateur or professional, knows and understands these principles and how to apply them. The successful application of the animation principles can elevate an animation from bland and inconsistent, to lively and interesting.

Below we will go over each animation principle and how it helps the animated performance. Take note that the order in which they are presented has nothing to do with the importance of the principle.  All principles are equally important to the process.

Lesson Duration: 4 hours

Please note that you will be expected to apply the following Principles of Animation throughout this course.

Topics:


Take note that the following definitions of the 12 Principles of Animation are in greater detail in the PDF file found in the Lesson Files compared to the animated video.

  • Solid Drawing
  • Staging
  • Straight Ahead Action and Pose to Pose
  • Squash and Stretch
  • Anticipation
  • Follow Through and Overlapping Action
  • Slow In and Slow Out
  • Arcs
  • Secondary Action
  • Timing
  • Exaggeration
  • Appeal

Task:

  • Take thorough notes on the 12 Principles of Animation and feel free to be creative by sketching some of the Principles of Animation as it will help you prepare for your certification exam at the end of this course.

  • Review the Principles of Animation PDF found in the Lesson Files, on the right of your screen.

  • Complete the Principles of Animation Techniques quiz.
  • Create an informational graphic poster on the 12 Principles of Animation and submit it below the quiz. Recommended size is 8.5 X 11 inches at 100 dpi. An example of the poster can be found in the Lesson Files, on the right of your screen. Review the Assessment rubric in the Lesson Files.
