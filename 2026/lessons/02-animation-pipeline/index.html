About this lesson

Understand the workflow behind an animated production

It is time to become familiar with the different animation workflows, how they end up merging together, and an overview of how they work with Toon Boom Harmony. An animation workflow refers to the steps and the order in which the different workflows occur when producing your movie or animated short.

No matter which method you choose, the pipeline is divided into three segments: pre-production, production, and post-production. Harmony is designed to handle part of pre-production and all of production, while post-production is managed through an external application.

The three-stage pipeline

The lesson organizes animation production into three major segments. The specific production steps change depending on the animation method.

01

Pre-Production

Establish the project before animation begins.

  • Script / synopsis
  • Storyboarding
  • Character, prop & location design
  • Audio recording
  • Animatic
  • Colour styling
  • Sound breakdown
02

Production

Create the animation using the chosen technique.

  • Traditional animation
  • Paperless animation
  • Cut-out animation
  • Layout & posing
  • Animation / clean-up
  • Ink & paint
  • Compositing
  • Export & render
03

Post-Production

Finish, assemble and distribute the production.

  • Final editing
  • Audio editing
  • Dubbing / post-synchro
  • Master distribution
Paperless Animation

Paperless animation consists of digitally drawing all images of an animation. It is similar to traditional animation, but the entire process uses digital tools while the animator still draws each frame individually.

Traditional Animation

Traditional animation is the original hand-drawn method, also called cel or classical animation. Drawings are created on paper, while modern colouring and compositing are commonly completed digitally.

Cut-Out Animation

Cut-out animation breaks a puppet into reusable pieces that are moved frame by frame. In digital cut-out animation, the parts are keyframed in animation software rather than redrawn for every frame.
